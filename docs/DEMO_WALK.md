# The Walk

### Six beats · about six minutes · rehearsed until boring

*Demo slice 2 (`demo/2-walk`). Narrative only — this document ships no code and changes no behaviour. Every figure below is **synthetic**, seeded by `seedDemoHousehold` through the real commands. No real household row appears in a demo, ever.*

---

## 0 · What this is

This is the script for showing Hearth to someone who is not in the household. It is not a feature tour and it is not a pitch deck read aloud. It is six things happening on two real devices, in one order, with the words already decided.

**The whole argument, in one sentence:** *two people, one month, two different jobs — and the app tells the truth about what it costs and who can move it.*

**How to use this document.** Read §1 and set the room up. Then run §3, beat by beat, out loud, four times before you show it to anybody. The fourth run is the one where it starts sounding like you instead of like a script. Beats 4 and 5 get run twice as often as the rest — §4 says why.

**The rule that makes the whole walk work:** *never explain a screen before it is on screen.* Put it up, say the beat line, then be quiet for two seconds while they read it. Hearth is a document; documents are read, not narrated.

## 1 · Before you walk

### 1.1 The two devices — both real

| Device | Member | Lands on | Why it must be real |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phone A** — yours | Jonathan | the desk | beats 2–4 and 6 |
| **Phone B** — a second physical phone | Bianca | **the Till** | beat 5 |

**Phone B is not a resized browser window and not a second tab.** The entire point of beat 5 is that a different person, on a different piece of glass, in a different room, is the only one who can move the money. A resized window says the opposite and everyone in the room knows it.

Set Phone B's landing surface to the Till before you start (member-owned landing preference, D-200). If Bianca's device opens on the desk in front of an audience, beat 5 has already leaked.

### 1.2 The household

- Environment: **Development**. Never Production. Never a real household.
- Seed: `seedDemoHousehold` — four months of history, built entirely through the real commands (Demo slice 1).
- "Today" for the walk: **12 September 2026**. Every figure in §3 is that date.
- Both phones on the same household, synced and quiet, **before** the audience is in the room.

### 1.3 The pre-flight, in order

1. Both phones charged, on the same network, brightness up, auto-lock off, notifications off.
2. Open the Charter on Phone A. Leave it there. Beat 1 begins with the app already open — no splash, no login, no "one second".
3. Open the Till on Phone B. Confirm the swipe button reads **"I spent something"** and the standing line reads **"Nothing has moved."**
4. Confirm the September figures on Phone A match §2 exactly. If a single one is off, **stop and fix the seed** — do not walk a month whose numbers you will have to talk around.
5. Confirm one motion is waiting on Phone B (Jonathan · contribution · $310.00) and that it has **not** already been confirmed by a rehearsal. Reseed if it has.
6. Say the closing line of beat 6 once, to the wall, before anyone arrives.

### 1.4 What you carry

Nothing. No slides, no laptop, no handout. Two phones, and the numbers in §2 in your head.

## 3 · The six beats

Times are the ceiling, not the target. If a beat runs long it is because you explained instead of pointing.

---

### Beat 1 — "This is a household, not an account." · ~45s

**On screen:** Phone A, the Charter, from the top.

**Say:**
> "This is the first screen. It's not a balance — it's what they agreed to."

Then read the purpose line off the page, in their words, and stop.

**Point at, in this order:**
1. The **purpose** at the top — the masthead, no label above it.
2. **"Bianca holds the money."** — one line, the custodian.
3. The split rule — its name, then her own sentence underneath it in quotes.
4. Scroll to the **signature block**. Two identical rules. Hers carries a date. His is blank.

**The line that lands:**
> "She signed. He hasn't. There's no badge, no red dot, no reminder, nothing counting at him. It's blank, and blank is true."

**Why it opens the walk:** nobody in this category opens on a document. Everyone opens on a balance or a chart. Opening on two names and an unsigned line tells the room in fifteen seconds that this is a different kind of object.

**The trap:** do not apologise for the unsigned line, do not say "we'd normally nudge him." The restraint *is* the feature. If you soften it here you will have to soften beat 6 too.

---

### Beat 2 — "One of them is paid by a clock." · ~40s

**On screen:** Phone A, the Course axis on the Month Spread, with the metronome.

**Say:**
> "Two incomes. One of them arrives on a schedule and one of them doesn't."

**Point at:**
- The **ticks** — regular, evenly spaced, the first one labelled `payday`. Her cadence.
- Then the **contribution marks** — irregular, different sizes, scattered. His.

**The line that lands:**
> "The ticks are timing, not money. They carry no amount — because what she puts in changes every month, and drawing it as a constant would be a lie. What's regular is *when*. What's irregular is *how much*."

**The trap:** do not call the ticks "her income." They are her paydays. The distinction is the whole reason the drawing is allowed to exist.

---

### Beat 3 — "So here's what your work paid for." · ~70s

**On screen:** Phone A, the register.

Let them read it for two full seconds before you say anything. It is a drawing; it does its own work.

**Say:**
> "One month. Every row is something the house owes. One scale across all of them, so a $128 bar and a $1,450 bar are the same ruler."

**Point at the rent bar and run left to right:**
> "Carried in from last month. Then hers. Then his. In the order the money actually arrived."

**Then run a finger down to the dashed tail:**
> "And that's not funded yet. It isn't red, it isn't a warning, it isn't an error. It's dashed, because nothing has gone wrong. It just hasn't been covered."

**The line that lands:**
> "There is no percentage on this screen. Nowhere in this product does it say she covered 62% and he covered 38%. That number would start a fight every single month, and it never tells you anything you can act on."

Then the totals under the rule: the month owes $3,075.00 · hers $1,960.00 · his $535.00 · carried $240.00 · not yet $340.00.

**The trap:** the temptation to say "so she's carrying more." Don't. You have just told them the product refuses to rank the two of them; do not do it yourself in the next breath.

---

### Beat 4 — "And here is what that costs." · ~90s · **rehearse twice**

**On screen:** Phone A, the Ask panel on the desk.

**Say — and let the figure sit alone for a moment:**
> **"September still needs $340.00."**

Then:
> "He's a tipped earner. His income isn't an input to this month — it's the output. The month needs a number, and he goes and earns it. So the question this product answers is one nobody else answers: *how much do I have to work to close this month?*"

**Point at the routes drawing:**
- The header: *bars are your safe number · whiskers reach the good night.*
- One route: **`15.5 hours · 2 nights`** — a Friday and a Saturday.
- The bar is the **safe** number. The whisker reaches the **good** night. The dashed copper line is the ask.
- The right column: `clears · $18 spare`.

**The line that lands:**
> "It never says *work two more shifts*. It lays two nights beside a number and lets him decide. And if it hasn't watched enough of his shifts to be honest, it doesn't guess — it says so and shows nothing."

**Then the turn — this is the beat.** Tap **the other door**:
> **"Or move Halifax to next month, and the ask is $40.00."**

> "Three hundred and forty dollars becomes forty. Every other product in this category, when you're short, tells you to earn more or spend less on groceries. This one offers *wanting less, later* — beside working more — every single time it shows a shortfall. Not behind a menu. Not only when the number is scary. Every time."

**And the fence:**
> "Tapping that doesn't move anything. It raises a motion. She still has to agree."

**The trap:** rushing to the other door. Let $340.00 be uncomfortable for a beat first. The door only lands if the room has felt the number.

---

### Beat 5 — "She's the one with the card." · ~75s · **rehearse twice**

**Put Phone A down. Pick up Phone B.** Do it visibly. Let the room see two objects.

**Say:**
> "Different person. Different phone. This is what she opens."

**On screen:** the Till.

**Point at:**
- The swipe button — **"I spent something"** — 96px tall, the largest thing on the screen, above everything.
- The standing line: **"Nothing has moved."**
- One line for the month: *the house has spent $2,735.00 so far.* No chart.
- At the foot: **`see everything`** — a plain link, always there.

**Say:**
> "This isn't a cut-down version of his screen. There's no upsell, no 'switch to the full app'. It's the correct surface for the person holding the card — and the door to everything is right there, permanently, if she wants it."

**Do the swipe — out loud, timed:**
1. Tap the button. The pad opens focused. Type the amount.
2. Tap a category cell. **It posts.**
3. The strip appears: **"Posted. Nothing moved."** with `Undo`.

> "Two taps. Ten seconds. One hand, standing at a counter. There is no third confirm step and there is no receipt photo."

**Then the motion.** Jonathan · contribution · **$310.00** · *"for the rent"*.

> "He proposed this. It's been sitting here. It is not in the balance."

Tap **Confirm**.

**The line that lands — say it slowly:**
> "*Nothing moved until she said so.* The money is in her savings account. Hearth cannot touch it. All Confirm does is agree that it happened."

Then point at **Hold**, the same size as Confirm, right beside it:
> "And if she isn't sure — Hold. Same size, same weight, not hidden in a menu. It doesn't say denied and it doesn't turn red. It says *let's talk about this*, and the card stays."

**The trap:** demoing Hold *instead of* Confirm because it is the more interesting button. Confirm is the beat — it is the money boundary. Show Hold as the thing beside it, in one sentence, and move on.

---

### Beat 6 — "The AI reads. It doesn't decide." · ~50s

**Back to Phone A.** Open the weekly.

**Say:**
> "There's an AI in here. Here's the whole of what it does."

**Point at Act 0, the reading** — three or four sentences.

**Tap one sentence.** The rows it came from open inline underneath.

> "Every sentence is tappable, and under every sentence are the exact rows it read. It quotes. It cites. It never proposes an amount and it never tells anyone to work."

> "And the weekly completes unsigned. One person reads it, one person stamps it, the other line stays blank. Nobody gets nagged."

**Close on this, verbatim, then stop talking:**
> **"Hearth cannot move a cent of this, and it cannot make anyone work a shift. It can only tell you the truth about both."**

**The trap:** adding a sentence after the closing line. There isn't one. Put the phone down.

## 4 · Timing, and what to rehearse

| Beat | Ceiling | Running |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 · the Charter | 0:45 | 0:45 |
| 2 · the metronome | 0:40 | 1:25 |
| 3 · the register | 1:10 | 2:35 |
| 4 · the ask and the other door | 1:30 | 4:05 |
| 5 · the Till | 1:15 | 5:20 |
| 6 · the reading | 0:50 | 6:10 |

**Beats 4 and 5 are the ones that close it.** Beat 4 is the only moment the product's actual question — *how much do I have to work?* — is on screen with a number attached. Beat 5 is the only moment the custody claim stops being a claim and becomes a thing they watched happen on a second phone. Everything else is setup for those two. Rehearse them twice as often as the rest, and rehearse them until they are boring — a walk that sounds excited about itself is a walk nobody believes.

## 5 · The questions you will be asked

Answer short. Do not upgrade any of these into a roadmap.

| They ask | You say |
|---|---|
| "Does it connect to the bank?" | "No. It records what two people agreed to. The money stays in her savings account and Hearth can't reach it." |
| "So it can't move money at all?" | "Correct. That's not a limitation we're working around — it's the design." |
| "Who covered more this month?" | "It won't tell you. Attribution and totals, yes. A percentage between two people, never — it's fenced in the code, not just in the copy." |
| "What if the AI is wrong about his shifts?" | "Then it refuses. It says how many shifts it's watched and shows nothing rather than guessing." |
| "What if she doesn't confirm?" | "Then it stays a proposal and it isn't in any balance. Or she holds it, and it says *let's talk about this*." |
| "Is there a dark mode?" | "No." |
| "Can he see the money?" | "He can see the household's month. He has no access to the account and he can't swipe." |
| "Can she see his hours?" | "No. His workload is never reported to her, anywhere." |
| "Is this real data?" | "No. Everything you're looking at is synthetic, seeded through the same commands the real app uses." |

## 6 · Never, during the walk

- **Never open a personal ledger.** No beat touches one. If a tap lands on one by accident, say "that's his own money, not the household's" and go back.
- **Never resize a browser to play Bianca.** Two phones or don't do beat 5.
- **Never say a percentage out loud**, including as a joke about the product not having one.
- **Never say** *lite*, *simple mode*, *basic*, *pending*, *overdue*, *action required*, or *denied*. If you describe the Till as "the simple one" you have undone beat 5 before you got there.
- **Never celebrate.** No "and look at that!" A covered month is a quiet `$0.00`.
- **Never apologise for the blank signature line, the empty state, or the refusal.** Each of those is the demo.
- **Never promise a cut feature** as "coming soon" — the Minute Book, receipt capture, bank feeds, plate customisation. "Not in this version" is a complete answer.
- **Never run it in Production, and never on a household with a real row in it.**

## 7 · When something breaks

| What happens | What you do |
|---|---|
| A figure doesn't match §2 | Stop the walk. Do not talk around it. "The seed's drifted — let me reset it" is a better look than improvising a number. |
| The register says *these rows don't tie* | Say the line out loud as a feature: "it just told me it can't reconcile, so it's showing me nothing instead of something wrong." Then move to beat 4. This is a genuinely good save. |
| Phone B has already confirmed the motion | Reseed before the room fills. Mid-walk, skip to the swipe and say "that one's already agreed." |
| Sync lags between the two phones | Say nothing. It resolves. Narrating latency is the only thing that makes latency memorable. |
| The routes drawing refuses | Read its refusal aloud — *"I've only watched 4 of your shifts. Ask me again in a few weeks — I'd be guessing."* — then go straight to the other door. The refusal is on-message. |
| Phone B dies | Beat 5 does not happen on Phone A. Say what it does, promise nothing, and go to beat 6. A described Till is worth more than a faked one. |

## 8 · Dry-run checklist

Run this once, straight through, before every showing.

- [ ] Development environment, synthetic seed, both phones on the same household
- [ ] Phone B lands on the Till without a tap
- [ ] September reads $3,075.00 / $340.00 / $40.00 — all three verified on screen
- [ ] The charter shows one signed line and one blank one, no badge anywhere
- [ ] The metronome shows ticks with no amounts, first one labelled `payday`
- [ ] The Halifax claim is present and unfunded, so the other door has something to offer
- [ ] One unconfirmed motion is waiting on Phone B
- [ ] The swipe completes in under fifteen seconds, hands only
- [ ] The weekly's Act 0 has at least one sentence that opens to rows
- [ ] The closing line said out loud, once, without reading it
- [ ] Total run under 6:30 without hurrying
