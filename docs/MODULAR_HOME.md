# A home made yours — local implementation

Implemented in `codex/modular-home`, based on `baff688d55e0994ad0c1c9ed578a4a4c49219197`. This is local implementation evidence, not deployment or physical-device acceptance.

## Ownership and location

Each member has one personal home, not one editable home per household. `personalLife.home` contains the layout, permanent awards, future blueprint, reusable arrangements and twelve renovation snapshots. Only `{memberId, plotId}` claims enter shared Hearthside state. First save claims the next free surveyed reserve through the existing command authority; subsequent saves use member-personal persistence. Other members cannot load or replace this personal document. Leaving/joining does not reorder claims. The seven existing reserves are the three Terraces and four Bight plots; an eighth claim fails and keeps the draft. No terrain was expanded to invent more land.

The original island house and existing rooms remain intact. The new homestead reads its position, rotation and elevation from the actual reserve. Its 52 × 32 metre interior building limit preserves the pad margin and front approach. This follows Jonathan's reserve-plot choice and subsequent “each user should have their own plot” instruction.

## Controls and entry points

Open **Renovation book** from Journey (both sizes), the reading edition, or the island toolbar. On the property, approach the drafting table and use its action or E. All entries open the same member's editor. **Visit my saved home** travels to that member's claim; the closest Journey zoom also enters the saved property. Existing budgeting tools remain directly available.

Choose Build, Furnish, Finishes, Garden, Storage, Blueprints & milestones, or Private journal. Select a room/object on the plan with pointer, Enter or Space. Coordinates move rooms in metre increments and furnishings in quarter metres. Rotate, rename, replace, store or restore the selection. Storing preserves identities and contents. Room presets and saved arrangements supply reusable starting points. Automatic placement finds a clear position; an item that cannot fit stays stored.

A project preview steps through foundation, frame, roof and finish. Undo/redo covers draft changes; Save is the explicit creative write. Future home accepts locked choices without changing the built home or finances, and can be brought into a building draft later. Saved history can be compared and restored as a draft, retaining more recent contents in storage. The editor pauses movement and vehicle controls; closing returns focus. Floor selection and walking cutaways reveal interiors. First-person keeps the physical walls.

## Catalogue and integration

Twenty architectural modules: starter cottage, porch, passage, reading room, library, kitchen, courtyard bar, pottery workshop, gallery, music room, conservatory, greenhouse, guest cottage, vehicle shed, bay-window room, balcony, upper room, observation nook, pergola and terrace. Four roof choices and seven coordinated finish collections. Thirty-four furniture/garden pieces cover seating, beds, tables, shelving, cabinets, lamps, rugs, curtains, books, plants, artwork, surfaces, counters, partitions, beds, paths, fences, gates and outdoor displays. Five arrangements provide starting sets.

Connected modules share doorway openings, lintels, foundations and trim. Upper rooms have actual stair treads and matching floor openings. Collision follows drawn walls, stairs and solid furniture; layout validation protects aisles, doors, stairs and the approach. The same geometry builder supplies full island art and simplified Journey volumes, roofs, finishes and gardens. Interiors are omitted at distant scale and resources are disposed on rebuild.

Books, conversation, Planner and Pottery Studio use existing routes and records, with Horizon return poses retained. The conservatory reuses the existing Mandevilla model, saved appearance and personal life evidence; it creates no second growth ledger. Pottery displays load the actual permitted design revision; a display is a reference, not another original. Private kept memories and unanimously kept shared memories are eligible only with lived experience where linked. Missing/withdrawn source content is not fabricated.

Music performance and functional vehicle parking inside the shed are not implemented; these are furnishable spaces. No boathouse is offered on inland plots. Roof/finish families are coordinated combinations, not a freeform mesh modeller. The editor is a selectable floor plan with numeric placement, not 3D drag-and-drop. Exports strip financial data, notes, evidence and original artwork references; blueprint import/publication and collaborative live editing are not implemented.

## Progression configuration

`src/home/progression.ts` owns all default thresholds and reward mappings. Accepted starter-budget receipts, accepted nonempty plans and member-reviewed distinct months provide historical credit. Shared and the member's own Personal records can qualify; partner-private records cannot. Distinct months deduplicate repeated imports/reviews. First-use budget or one review unlocks established finishes; one review reading; three garden; two hosting; three workshop; accepted evidenced follow-through or four reviews architecture; 25% backed progress on a selected goal or six reviews sanctuary; an authority-indexed fired Studio creation or two reviews gallery. Existing Nest firing records also qualify; old standalone documents without completion metadata need their next accepted Studio edit to refresh that index.

Upcoming-commitment and income workflows lack durable dedicated completion receipts, so their defaults use the equivalent review paths. Goals require actual backing, not scheduled payments. Grants are permanent and recomputed inside the accepting command; client-supplied awards are rejected. Corrected evidence changes current eligibility without confiscating prior awards. No balances, transactions, goals or commitments are written by renovation.

## Validation and release limits

Focused domain, UI, private sync, return-routing and world toolbar regression checks; actual App browser checks across all three themes at desktop and phone widths; actual Horizon walker entry and upper stair traversal on the second user's plot. All use synthetic local households with outbound services blocked. Source tests are under `test/home-*.test.ts`; browser evidence is in `/tmp/hearth-home-browser` and `/tmp/hearth-home-island`.

The final local build passed, including asset validation, application/workspace TypeScript and the production bundle. The High quick gate passed 1,181 assertions but failed two stale boating-storage source fences and exceeded its five-minute target at 317.451 seconds. The fences were corrected without admitting financial writers or network calls; the subsequent focused run passed all 42 domain/editor/source-fence tests. This does **not** convert the earlier gate into a clean pass.

The final feature-focused rerun passed **54/54 tests across five files in 38.78 seconds**, including the real Journey → Renovation book → Visit flow, ready books throughout, actual stairs, distinct owner plot, full viewport and the existing travel toolbar. The selected broader serial run passed 219/222 assertions; subsequent focused rechecks passed the two-client real local authority test and presentation-flag-off Plan route. The remaining legacy navigation/theme rehearsal reached its theme phase but timed out at 180 seconds; it is not counted as passed. Old Record/Boathouse/Glasshouse selectors were brought up to date, and the legacy presentation fixture explicitly isolates its feature flags and cache. Direct heavy-world startup also encountered the local-books startup deadline under this run's load; the proven home visit begins from ready Journey. Logs: `/tmp/hearth-home-final-focused.log`, `/tmp/hearth-home-browser-recheck.log`, `/tmp/hearth-home-legacy-final.log`.

A valid extensive layout with ten rooms and 95 placed furnishings was measured alongside the starter cottage. Ten local CPU construction samples gave median build times of 1.58 / 2.07 ms for starter map/full detail, and 13.58 / 31.42 ms for extensive map/full detail. The extensive home used 341 / 600 meshes and 3,916 / 7,216 triangles; every observed geometry/material resource received disposal. One thousand collision-plus-floor queries took 66–182 ms. Evidence: `/tmp/hearth-home-measurements.json`. These are CPU construction/collision measurements, not sustained GPU frame-rate or physical-device acceptance.

The existing scoped command and private continuity channels are reused. Hosted two-device saves, real Google sessions and atomic cross-device plot-claim races have not been proven here. Local revision conflicts are covered; no simultaneous editing promise is made. Household lighting and island time drive the model, with seasonal vegetation colour and a simple winter roof accent; precipitation-specific snow accumulation/wet-surface art is still absent. Physical iPhone/Mac feel, screen-reader review, sustained extensive-property GPU performance and final art acceptance remain open. No deployment, schema application or meaningful household mutation was performed.
