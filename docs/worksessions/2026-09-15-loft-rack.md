## Household outcome

The loft's one ledge becomes a rack. Each shelf is a weight: a brass block on its right end that slides along the board — further right, heavier, so a higher, heavier shelf takes more of a pour. Each shelf has a post at its left with a pin that rides up it: the fill mark, where that shelf's banks stop taking (the tide shows as a dashed line on every cat of the shelf). Between two banks on a shelf stands a slim brass divider; slide it and the shelf's share splits unevenly between them. A dotted peg hangs another shelf below; an empty shelf can be taken down. Banks go along a shelf or down onto another by drag (or Shift+Arrow). Beneath the rack stands the jug — the Fund's safe surplus — and tilting it reads, in one breath, exactly what would land where; *Pour it* opens Confirm and posts the month-end rollover through the command the Fund already has. Nothing here is a form: every number is a thing you slide, and every reading is words.

Jonathan's ask (2026-09-15): "i think we need more of a shelving system than just one shelf. the different shelves should act as weights. higher might get weight 5 while the lowest shelf might get weight 1. or by percentage. You should be able to choose a cutoff point for funding on each shelf and assign how much is being split up automatically both horizontally or vertically. this should not be done with information and inputs, but through physical touch and interactivity." To the four choices offered (what pours, how a shelf weighs, what a cutoff is, how a shelf splits): "3 but we can't make it rigid it needs to be adjustable" — a weight per shelf on fixed rungs, but the shelves themselves hung and taken down; the defaults for the rest: the Fund's safe surplus by default, tilted to the amount; a fill mark; a divider you drag.

## What changed

## Decisions and interpretations

- **The rack is a plan, not money.** It lives on the Build plan bank's household design row beside the order it replaces, and writes that order for older builds. The only money act is the pour, which is the Fund's own rollover command, behind the same Confirm, by the same custodian.
- **A weight is a rung (1–10), read as "5 of 11".** Jonathan asked for weight or percentage; a share of the rack's total is both at once and never needs a figure typed. A newly hung shelf hangs one lighter than the lightest, so the top shelf is fed first by default.
- **The pin is a fill mark in twentieths, not a dollar cutoff.** A dollar figure would be a form; the tide on the cats is the reading. At the crown the shelf takes to full; at the foot it takes nothing.
- **The jug shows a figure.** The ledge's rule (D-256) — no amount on the shelf — holds for the shelf, the weights, the pins and the sub line; the proof asserts it. But a pour is a money act and Confirm needs its figure, so the Fund's safe surplus and the tilted split are said in the jug and in the line, which are the Fund's, not the shelf's.
- **The pour previews exact cents, the odd cent landing whole**, so what Confirm says is what the command posts.
- **Only a bank, a pin, a weight and a divider hold the hand.** A grab on a bare board or the jug is still the house's haul, so the loft can still be hauled out of on a phone that the rack fills.
- **The line's standing hint gives way in a short room** — and the house proof's stand-in chrome at 320×568 leaves the rack a strip; the actual App there already stands the loft under a books banner (queen-house evidence), so the hand-work is proved from 320×700 up.
- **D-261** is `habitat-member-mapping.patch` (#483); `main` carries **D-262** twice (the kitty jars row, #486, and the Our Path world section, #488); this is **D-263**.

## Remaining uncertainty

- Two shelves fit the 1100×800 office and 390/720 phones without the rack scrolling; three or more scroll inside the rack on short frames (by design — the house never scrolls). At 320×700 two shelves scroll by 8px.
- The 3D boards follow the drawn shelves in SwiftShader stills; a phone's GPU has not drawn them.
- A rack stored by this build is refused by an older build's `shapeKittyNestDesigns` ("needs an updated Hearth") — the same rule every design field lives by, but the first time a room writes one.
- The nest's own order of two fresh goals is not fixed (ids are generated), so the proofs read the shelf by name; a rack, once written, fixes it.
- The pour reads `safeRolloverCents` on `today`; the Fund's own board may say a different safe figure on a day the projection changes.
- Data/environment: fictional fixtures only; no hosted, schema, Production or real-household writes.
