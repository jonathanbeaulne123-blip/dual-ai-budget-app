## Start here
- `Hearth_The_Horizon_Pitch_Deck.pdf` — 47-page reading/presentation edition.
- `Hearth_The_Horizon_Pitch_Deck.pptx` — PowerPoint with editable text/layout and detailed speaker notes; diagrams are embedded images, with the masterplan SVG supplied separately.
- `Hearth_The_Horizon_Review.html` — self-contained offline review: slide navigation, readable notes, source register, and a map lab with route filters and seven clickable building anchors. Open it in a browser.

## Map handoff
`Horizon_Masterplan.pdf/.png/.svg` includes the masterplan legend and real place names. Separate vehicle, skate, pedestrian and water images use the same layout. `Horizon_Concept_Manifest.json` contains the proposal’s anchors and route control points.

The JSON is a DESIGN HANDOFF, not an application schema or import-ready WorldDefinition. It must not be loaded into the live app as navigation, collision or financial state.

## What this is
A source-grounded proposal for the user-selected “01 The Horizon” island. It keeps seven actual building hosts, twelve existing place IDs, and the three requested movement networks. Exact placement, scale, grades, vehicle envelope, crossings, the west dry accent and the reservoir display separation remain design proposals.

Source screenshots come from the supplied Mountain Dissection PDF. Repository references are pinned to `2d9c21923c261ae14914eee3b1cbf908d3ec2fe7`. The review is a historical snapshot; parallel changes must be reconciled before implementation. No repository changes, deployments, live data modifications or Horizon gameplay tests were performed.

## Visual status
The selected Horizon illustration is the visual north star. New maps, cutaways and ground-level images in the deck are intentionally labelled concept/planning sketches. They are not finished environment art, replacement Mandevilla models or runtime captures.

## Source trail
`Sources_and_Design_Notes.md` and the PowerPoint speaker notes provide per-slide rationale and full source locations. No font files or external runtime dependencies are bundled.

## Slide-by-slide design rationale

### 01. The Horizon
Chapter: WORLD DISSECTION / DESIGN PITCH
Sources: REF, BRIEF, G1
The user chose 01 The Horizon, not the archipelago or sanctuary. This deck develops that direction into a source-grounded, spatially specific proposal. The approved illustration remains a reference; the new map is a planning diagram. No production code or user financial state was modified.

### 02. Seven buildings. An entire world.
Chapter: 00 / THE PROPOSITION
Sources: G1, BRIEF, REF
G1 supplies the count of seven actual app buildings. The outdoor Court and Campfire are not extra buildings. Transport shelters, bridges and a lookout are landscape infrastructure, not invented app destinations. The pitch preserves open space and distributes the existing places rather than filling the approved illustration’s town literally.

### 03. What is decided—and what this deck proposes
Chapter: 00 / THE PROPOSITION
Sources: BRIEF, MD pp.6–17, G8
The new layout, heights, route widths, named recreation lines, narrative walkthroughs and visual treatment are recommendations for discussion. Source defects are treated as historical findings. Jonathan said the other information is being worked on; this deck does not claim fixes are missing today. Snapshot pinning prevents accidental mixing with unseen concurrent changes.

### 04. Keep the openness—not the illustrated town
Chapter: 00 / THE PROPOSITION
Sources: WII, REF, G1
Read the images for the balance of land and landmarks. The latest user-selected image still contains a decorative town; seven actual buildings, not every painted roof, are the binding inventory for this proposal. The mountain is a northern visual anchor, not the shape of the whole map.

### 05. From a stacked mountain to a connected landscape
Chapter: 00 / THE VISUAL DISSECTION
Sources: MD pp.6,9, REF
The Mountain Dissection page 6 diagram and page 9 aerial show stacked traverses and destinations distributed by altitude. This slide’s interpretation is compositional, not a re-run of the source’s defect audit. The proposal creates a lower populated landscape, a smaller remote highland mass, a loop road and a connected water corridor.

### 06. First 60 seconds: a welcome, not a flyover
Chapter: 01 / FIRST 60 SECONDS
Sources: G2, G3, BRIEF
The scene begins at the existing Court, at useful character height. The mailbox and familiar Queen remain the source-supported orientation objects. The four beats are proposed pacing, not measured launch times. The person may skip the entire scenic sequence and open the existing tool owner immediately. Do not counterfeit live partner presence or real activity to make the world busy.

### 07. The Horizon masterplan
Chapter: 02 / LANDSCAPE & PLACEMENT
Sources: G1, BRIEF
The plan uses a conceptual metre grid with north at the top. Positions, heights and route controls are explicitly provisional. B01 Our home: lower meadow. B02 Fund bank: Court edge. B03 Library: woodland edge. B04 Glasshouse: lakeside meadow. B05 Studio: dry/meadow transition. B06 Cottage: orchard. B07 Boathouse: sheltered south river/harbour approach. The JSON manifest is a handoff description, not an app-importable WorldDefinition.

### 08. Seven buildings become twelve existing places
Chapter: 02 / LANDSCAPE & PLACEMENT
Sources: G1, G2, G3
Count method: Our home carries kitchen, tower, cellar and atlas (four physical places inside one building). Six other buildings contribute bank, library, glasshouse, kiln, cottage and boathouse. Court and Campfire are outdoor places. This totals 12 Harbour place IDs using 7 buildings. Physical maps follow VILLAGE_SITES/SITE_FOR_PLACE rather than stale descriptive comments about the atlas roof.

### 09. Elevation should create moments—not a ladder
Chapter: 02 / TERRAIN DISSECTION
Sources: MD pp.6–10, REF
The source review critiques equal altitude bands and a plateau-cone construction. These proposed height bands are starting values for a hand-authored heightfield, not a validated terrain calculation. Foundations, ramps, river banks and corridors must be solved together in 3D. Do not inflate the world merely to reach a coordinate target.

### 10. Different biomes. Different reasons to linger.
Chapter: 02 / TERRAIN DISSECTION
Sources: REF, BRIEF, MD pp.10–13
The dry plateau is a proposed extension of Horizon’s west coast responding to the user’s requested desert/plateau diversity. The biomes blend by terrain and planting; they are not nearest-centre tints or perfect rings around buildings. Large snow regions from the other concepts are not included. Each biome combines geometry, plant families, light, ground material and spatial rhythm.

### 11. From source to sea—with a truthful Fund instrument
Chapter: 02 / TERRAIN DISSECTION
Sources: G4, MD pp.7,10
Existing BasinReading projects the shared Fund’s operating balance, Kitty reserves, free-to-spend, pending contributions and confirmed flows. It does not equate generic expenses or a worked shift with a confirmed bank movement. This pitch proposes placing a contained, labelled chamber beside the dam while the natural lake and river remain ambience. That is a material presentation change, not an existing implemented rule. Scale changes and unknown/freshness states must be legible.

### 12. Open space is part of the product
Chapter: 02 / TERRAIN DISSECTION
Sources: BRIEF, G1, REF
Open space is not a backlog of buildings. Keep visual corridors, future-friendly but unpromised clearings, and quiet land that does not require a task or reward. These are compositional targets rather than measured area percentages. A no-props terrain review and repeated eye-height screenshots should establish the right balance before decoration.

### 13. Three networks—not three colours on the same path
Chapter: 03 / MOVEMENT
Sources: BRIEF
These sections translate Jonathan’s three route types into physical design. The road is genuinely paved, broad and unobstructed for future vehicles. The skate corridor offers embodied speed control and optional route choices integrated into terrain and supported architecture. The pedestrian network is board-free and vehicle-free, with an alternative to stairs where a required destination would otherwise be inaccessible. Dimensions are gameplay blockout targets, not civil engineering standards.

### 14. V01 / Horizon Drive
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: BRIEF, MD pp.6–9
V01 is a lower island loop. V02 is a highland spur ending at a summit approach, not a road forced through every destination. Short arrival spurs stop behind gardens; they do not put vehicles through the Court. Concept targets: 8 m running surface, 1 m shoulder each side, 5 m clear height, gentle typical grades around 4–6%, review sections above 8%. These need vehicle steering, clearance and gradient validation. The two labelled tunnels and gorge bridge are reservations, not implemented structures.

### 15. Bridge, tunnel, doorstep: three different structures
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: MD pp.7–8,17, BRIEF
The review’s historical screenshots and descriptions motivate the distinction between rendered ribbons and inhabited structures. A bridge needs thickness and support visible from below, plus appropriate edge collision. A tunnel needs a roof/ceiling and a camera-compatible interior. A destination arrival needs a clear vehicle stop and a door-facing pedestrian route. Reuse the existing surface/collision contracts while re-authoring geometry and arrival relationships.

### 16. Skate the island—not a track pasted onto it
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: BRIEF, MD p.8
S1 Meadow Roll is the forgiving main descent from the lake terrace to the south landing. S2 Makers’ Weave moves through the Library’s service side, studio terrace and orchard edge, using a ground bypass and optional supported gallery. S3 Gorge Line is a later skill route along the east valley, with a deliberately grade-separated road crossing. The diagram is topology/intent; it is not a validated 3D skating surface. Finishes stop before pedestrian destinations and need sufficient runout.

### 17. Shape the speed, then tune the controller
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: BRIEF, MD p.8
This is an authored experience profile, not a simulated speed curve. Use deliberate grades, broad banked curves, surface response and player-controlled braking. Optional branches split with sight distance and rejoin with room to see other movement. Main-line width is proposed at 3–4 m; reunions and braking pockets 5–6 m. Every jump or grind must be optional; a forgiving ground alternative must remain. None of these values is a real-world skating safety recommendation.

### 18. Hide the play inside useful architecture
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: BRIEF, MD pp.8,12, G1
The optional route is built into the side retaining terrace and gallery, not floated near the Library and called its roof. The ordinary entrance remains clear, and the reading courtyard is pedestrian-only. The support columns and roof clearance belong to the same authored geometry used by collision. A lower bypass shares the entry and reunion; neither line changes the underlying Books tool. The sketch is diagrammatic and does not replace the existing Library model without an asset review.

### 19. The quiet network is not the leftover network
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: BRIEF, G1
Four primary quiet routes are proposed, supplemented by actual door approaches and a short summit walk. W1 connects Court, bank and home. W2 joins the Library and Glasshouse through orchard and woodland. W3 stays close to the gorge and water. W4 follows dunes and the southern Campfire. W6 is a direct pedestrian connector between the core and Library. Crossing or arriving from a vehicle or board requires a clear, deliberate park/dismount action; do not silently transform the user’s mode or leave an invisible wall. Required app access remains available through quick tools.

### 20. A crossing on the map is not a collision in the world
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: BRIEF, MD pp.7–8,17
X01 is a conceptual grade-separated interchange, not a completed engineering section. The vehicle road crosses high on a supported bridge; the skate line uses a lower supported shelf/underpass; the pedestrian path stays riverside. Piers, railings, camera clearance and visible geometry must use the same surface/collision definition. Any 2D intersection elsewhere in the plan must be resolved explicitly as separated or a controlled dismount/arrival threshold before it is treated as traversable.

### 21. Transport should give us another way to see the island
Chapter: 03 / ROADS & ROUTES
Sources: MD pp.5,14,17, G8
The source contains gondola and funicular systems. This pitch recommends a cross-gorge gondola alignment G1 and a separate short highland funicular F1, but their exact stops and physics must be reconciled with parallel transport work. The stations are modest infrastructure, not additional app buildings. Boarding by proximity, explicit rider attachment and a dedicated scenic camera are acceptance needs drawn from the source audit, not assertions that they remain broken.

### 22. Scale the world by chosen journeys
Chapter: 03 / MOVEMENT & ACCESS
Sources: G3, G8, BRIEF
The world metre grid is a provisional blockout scale. Route lengths below are simple sums between authored control points, so they are lower-resolution estimates rather than final spline arc lengths. Times use explicitly assumed average speeds with no stops; they are not measured performance. V01 about 1,786 m / 12 m/s = 149 s. S1 about 490 m / 6 m/s = 82 s. W1 about 184 m / 1.7 m/s = 108 s. These are calibration examples, not requirements to perform daily tasks. Open a money tool from anywhere and restore scene context afterwards.
