# Main road: BEFORE, what a driver sees

Set `before` at main `ae27718` (`claude/road-before`). Captured with `node scripts/horizon/capture-road.mjs --set before --out docs/horizon/evidence/road/before` in 445 s. Headless Chromium used SwiftShader at 1280×800, `tier=full`, on 2026-06-21. Day shots are at 13:00 and night shots at 22:30. This is not device evidence. Frame times in `captures.json` are SwiftShader times, not device performance.

**Driver poses** are look poses, not the vehicle camera. The eye sits on the baked bed centreline, 2 m into the right lane, 1.9 m above the surface `geography.surface` reports at that point. It looks 40 m ahead along the bed with a vertical FOV of 58°. **Ride shots** use the real controller and cameras: `restore` at [1400,24,1060], `toggleCruiser`, then 6 s of `input` + `simulateMotion` with scripted steering. That covered 66 m at a maximum of 12.8 m/s with 0 contacts, followed by braking. The capture reported one error: a 404 for `/favicon.ico` on page load, which has no effect on the pictures.

I also sampled all three beds against the live geography, with no pictures. This was a one-off check, not part of the script:
- **Terrain above the deck:** V01 has 13 of 3696 samples, 0.64 m at most. The worst run is at [1519,1027], points 40–44, which is the harbour-east pose. V03 shows it through its tunnel, which is expected. VG has 1 sample.
- **Ground more than 1.25 m below the deck at ±6 m:** V01 has 320 of 2464 edge samples, 24 m at most, including bridges. VG has 42, 15.5 m at most.

## Across the whole route
- **One material and colour for everything.** Deck, shoulders, kerbs, junction aprons and pads are all the same pale #c9c2b4-ish stone. The road has no markings of any kind: no centre line, no edge line, nothing at junctions. At driver height the lane, the shoulder and the footway beside it all read as one big pale slab. The only visible lines are thin slab seams and dark kerb shadows.
- **Parallel strips.** Across most of the Prow, the Crown and the north (06, 07, 08, 09), a second pale strip runs beside V01, split off by a kerb gap. By position this is the Year Walk footway; I did not confirm it in code. From the driver's seat it reads as a second carriageway or a road split in two.
- **Stepped edge pieces.** Parapet and retaining pieces decided every 2.5 eu show as staircase blocks down embankments (12, 16, 18, 19, 22) and as vertically offset kerb segments (09). They look like debris, not a designed edge.
- **Yellow slabs in the carriageway.** Flat yellow threshold/crossing marker slabs sit inside the lane (05, 11, 16, 17). At 16 a dark wedge beside one reads as a small lip. At night these are the only lit things, as a warm pool with a floating glow bead in the lane (27, 28).
- **No roadside planting on Horizon land.** No trees, shrubs or flowers anywhere along V01, V03 or VG. The only vegetation is inside the Mountain v2 region (20, 34), which also shows the quality gap the brief names.
- **Terrain shapes.** Cliff and rock terrain beside the road renders as large striped cones and sawtooth spires (06, 07, 22) and as vertical striped walls right at the kerb (02, 03, 05). It reads as heightfield stair-stepping, not rock.
- **Night.**
  - No streetlights. The surface is unlit, a uniform slate blue.
  - Kerbs, parapets and retaining walls switch to a bright white night face colour, the "chalk" solids. That makes the route legible but graphic, like a wireframe.
  - The only light sources are the small threshold lamp beads and their pools.
- **Tunnel floors are blue.** The Prow gallery (03, 04) and the Mountain Road tunnel (18, 19) have a floor that reads as water, not road. The cause is unverified; it may be the tunnel floor's surface colour.
- **Camera clipping:** none seen in any shot, including the three ride cameras.

## Could not judge from stills
- Whether guards are continuous where drops exist (07, 08, 34). Only the numeric sample above covers this.
- Collision agreement with visible edges, and whether the dark wedges at 16 and 11 are real lips.
- Lane width in metres at driver height.
- Z-fighting versus deliberate two-tone decking on the Bight Bridge (12, 13). That needs motion.
- Weather: there is no weather system on main.
- Real-time frame rate: SwiftShader ran at roughly 0.3–2 s per frame.
